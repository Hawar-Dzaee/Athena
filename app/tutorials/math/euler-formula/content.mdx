# Euler's Formula

Euler's formula connects the exponential function to trigonometry:

$$
e^{i\theta} = \cos\theta + i\sin\theta
$$

The real part is $\cos\theta$ and the imaginary part is $\sin\theta$.
As $\theta$ sweeps from $0$ to $2\pi$, the point $e^{i\theta}$ traces the **unit circle** in the complex plane.

## The constant $c$

Multiplying by a complex constant $c$ transforms the circle:

$$
c \cdot e^{i\theta}
$$

- If $c$ is a **real number** (like $2$ or $0.5$), it **scales** the radius — the circle gets bigger or smaller.
- If $c = e^{i\varphi}$ (like $e^{i\pi/4}$), it **rotates** the starting point by $\varphi$ radians — the circle stays the same size but begins at a different angle.
- You can combine both: $2 \cdot e^{i\pi/4}$ scales the radius to $2$ *and* rotates by $45°$.
