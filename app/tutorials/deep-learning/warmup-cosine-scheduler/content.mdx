# Warmup Cosine Scheduler

Most modern training runs don't use a fixed learning rate. They ramp up,
then smoothly decay. The **warmup cosine schedule** is the most common
pattern — used in vision transformers, language models, and contrastive
learning alike.

The idea is simple: start slow, peak, then coast down to near-zero
following a cosine curve. But the details matter — too short a warmup
destabilizes early training, too long wastes capacity on a cautious ramp.

## Why warmup?

At initialization, weights are random and gradients are noisy. A large
learning rate on the first few steps can push the network into a region
it can't recover from — loss spikes, gradients explode, and training
diverges.

**Warmup** solves this by linearly increasing the learning rate from a
small value (often zero) to the target peak over a set number of steps:

$$
\eta_t = \eta_{\text{peak}} \cdot \frac{t}{T_{\text{warmup}}}, \quad t \leq T_{\text{warmup}}
$$

This gives the optimizer time to estimate gradient statistics (especially
important for Adam-family optimizers whose running averages start cold)
before taking full-sized steps.

## The cosine decay

After warmup, the learning rate follows a cosine curve from the peak
down to a minimum value:

$$
\eta_t = \eta_{\text{min}} + \frac{1}{2}(\eta_{\text{peak}} - \eta_{\text{min}}) \left(1 + \cos\!\left(\pi \cdot \frac{t - T_{\text{warmup}}}{T_{\text{total}} - T_{\text{warmup}}}\right)\right)
$$

Why cosine and not linear? Cosine keeps the learning rate near the peak
for longer in the middle of training (where most useful learning
happens), then accelerates the decay toward the end. The initial descent
is gentle, the final descent is steep — and the transition is smooth,
with no discontinuities.

## The full schedule

Combining both phases:

$$
\eta_t = \begin{cases}
\eta_{\text{peak}} \cdot \dfrac{t}{T_{\text{warmup}}} & t \leq T_{\text{warmup}} \\[8pt]
\eta_{\text{min}} + \dfrac{1}{2}(\eta_{\text{peak}} - \eta_{\text{min}})\!\left(1 + \cos\!\left(\pi \cdot \dfrac{t - T_{\text{warmup}}}{T_{\text{total}} - T_{\text{warmup}}}\right)\right) & t > T_{\text{warmup}}
\end{cases}
$$

## Where you'll see this

- **Vision Transformers (ViT, DeiT):** 5–10 epochs of warmup out of 300
  total, cosine decay to zero.
- **BERT / GPT pretraining:** warmup over the first 1–2% of steps,
  cosine or linear decay for the rest.
- **Contrastive learning (SimCLR, BYOL, VICReg):** 10 epochs of warmup,
  cosine decay — the warmup prevents early representation collapse.
- **Fine-tuning:** shorter warmup (often just 5–10% of steps) since the
  model starts from a good initialization.

## Key takeaways

- **Warmup stabilizes early training** by giving optimizers time to
  accumulate gradient statistics before taking large steps.
- **Cosine decay lingers near the peak** then drops steeply — this
  allocates more training budget to the productive middle phase.
- **The warmup fraction is a design choice.** Pretraining from scratch
  needs more warmup; fine-tuning needs less.
- **Minimum LR > 0** can help — a tiny residual learning rate prevents
  the optimizer from fully stalling at the end of training.
