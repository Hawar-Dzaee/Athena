## The equation

A continuous random variable $X$ follows a Gaussian distribution $X \sim \mathcal{N}(\mu, \sigma^2)$
if its **probability density function** (PDF) is:

$$
f(x) = \frac{1}{\sigma\sqrt{2\pi}} \, \exp\!\left(-\frac{(x - \mu)^2}{2\sigma^2}\right)
$$

Two parameters control everything:

- **$\mu$ (mu)** — the **mean**, the center of the bell curve. Shifting $\mu$
  slides the entire curve left or right without changing its shape.
- **$\sigma$ (sigma)** — the **standard deviation**, a measure of spread. A
  small $\sigma$ gives a tall, narrow peak; a large $\sigma$ gives a short, wide
  one. The total area under the curve is always $1$.

## The isotropic Gaussian

The interactive plot above already shows a bivariate Gaussian — switch to 3D
and notice that each axis gets its own spread $\sigma_x$ and $\sigma_y$.
The full distribution is described by a **covariance matrix**:

$$
\Sigma = \begin{bmatrix} \sigma_x^2 & 0 \\ 0 & \sigma_y^2 \end{bmatrix}
$$

A multivariate Gaussian is **isotropic** when every dimension shares the same
variance and there are no correlations:

$$
\Sigma = \sigma^2 \, \mathbf{I}
$$

One scalar $\sigma$ replaces the entire matrix. The PDF simplifies to:

$$
f(\mathbf{x}) = \frac{1}{(2\pi\sigma^2)^{d/2}} \, \exp\!\left(-\frac{\|\mathbf{x} - \boldsymbol{\mu}\|^2}{2\sigma^2}\right)
$$

$f$ depends only on the **distance** $\|\mathbf{x} - \boldsymbol{\mu}\|$ from the
mean — not on direction. Contours of equal probability are perfect circles in 2D
(spheres in 3D). Toggle between **Isotropic** and **Anisotropic** to watch circles
squash into ellipses:
