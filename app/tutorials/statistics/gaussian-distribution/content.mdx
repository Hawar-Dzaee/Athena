# Gaussian Distribution

The **Gaussian** (or **normal**) distribution is the most important distribution in
statistics. It shows up everywhere: measurement errors, heights of people, pixel
noise, gradient updates in neural networks.

## The equation

A continuous random variable $X$ follows a Gaussian distribution $X \sim \mathcal{N}(\mu, \sigma^2)$
if its **probability density function** (PDF) is:

$$
f(x) = \frac{1}{\sigma\sqrt{2\pi}} \, \exp\!\left(-\frac{(x - \mu)^2}{2\sigma^2}\right)
$$

Two parameters control everything:

- **$\mu$ (mu)** — the **mean**, the center of the bell curve. Shifting $\mu$
  slides the entire curve left or right without changing its shape.
- **$\sigma$ (sigma)** — the **standard deviation**, a measure of spread. A
  small $\sigma$ gives a tall, narrow peak; a large $\sigma$ gives a short, wide
  one. The total area under the curve is always $1$.

## Interactive plot

Drag the sliders to change $\mu$ and $\sigma$ and watch the bell curve reshape in
real time. The shaded bands show how probability concentrates around the mean:

- **±1σ** contains ≈ **68.27%** of the total probability
- **±2σ** contains ≈ **95.45%**
- **±3σ** contains ≈ **99.73%**
