# AdaptiveAvgPool2d

Most pooling layers ask you to specify a **kernel size** and **stride**.
`nn.AdaptiveAvgPool2d` flips the interface: you specify the **output size**
you want, and PyTorch figures out the kernel and stride for you.

$$
\text{out}(i,j) = \frac{1}{|R_{ij}|}\sum_{(m,n)\in R_{ij}} \text{input}(m,n)
$$

where $R_{ij}$ is the rectangular region of input cells that map to output
position $(i,j)$. The region boundaries are computed as:

$$
\text{start}_k = \left\lfloor \frac{k \cdot H_{\text{in}}}{H_{\text{out}}} \right\rfloor, \qquad
\text{end}_k = \left\lfloor \frac{(k+1) \cdot H_{\text{in}}}{H_{\text{out}}} \right\rfloor
$$

This is exactly what sits at the end of every modern classifier
(ResNet, EfficientNet, ViT hybrid heads) right before the fully-connected
layer: `AdaptiveAvgPool2d((1, 1))` collapses any spatial resolution down to a
single value per channel.

## Why "adaptive"?

A regular `AvgPool2d(kernel_size=7)` only works if the input is exactly 7
pixels wide. If you resize your input images, the pooling breaks.
`AdaptiveAvgPool2d((1, 1))` doesn't care — it works on *any* spatial size,
because it computes the window on the fly.

This decoupling is why you can take a ResNet trained on 224$\times$224
images and fine-tune it on 448$\times$448 inputs without changing the
architecture: the adaptive pool absorbs the difference.
